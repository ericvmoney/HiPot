      How this board is built

      Every role comes from the company's own job board. The board reads each company's public job feed and links each role to the posting there. Nothing comes from an aggregator or a search result. A role that leaves the company's board leaves this one at the next refresh. New marks a role first seen in the last three business days (Monday to Friday, Mountain time); the roles found the first time a company's board is read are not marked new.

      Companies are screened for climate fit before their roles appear, under a written rubric.

      The board opens on clean tech. Companies one step outside it, such as robotics, aerospace, semiconductors, defense, mining or oil and gas, are listed too, each under its category; tick them in the Category filter to see their roles. Their cards name the category instead of a climate tier. Clean tech itself is decided by the climate screen below.

      Startups and established companies are both listed, and labelled. A startup here is independent, has fewer than 4,000 people, and still shows a sign of growth: founded in the last 15 years, a funding round in the last 5, or listed on a stock exchange for less than 5. Established climate companies are listed too; use the Company type filter to see only one kind.

      Pay vs market tags compare a role's published pay range with the published ranges of comparable roles on this board: the same role group, level and pay period, in the role's metro area, or across the US when its metro area has fewer than five. "Above" and "below" mean the midpoint is more than 10 percent from their median midpoint. It compares employers' published ranges; it is not a salary estimate and says nothing about any candidate. The pay ranges page shows the same groups.

      Ranking for your persona uses plain rules, not an AI model. It ranks roles for you, never people. A role scores up to 100 points: kind of work 25, discipline 25 (your first discipline counts most), level 20, place 15, a sector you prefer 8, a company stage you prefer 7. A role in a discipline next to one of yours, such as test next to controls, earns part of the discipline points; a technical role in a discipline far from all of yours, such as cloud software for a controls engineer, earns none and only half the points for kind of work. If you said you will not relocate, an on-site role elsewhere loses 10 points instead of gaining any for place. Remote means remote in the US: a role whose only places are in other countries gains nothing for place. A persona for non-technical work, such as supply chain, needs no discipline; then discipline counts the same for every role. Pay counts only when the employer published it: a role with no published pay loses 3 points, no more. Companies you left out are hidden, and so are companies whose only sectors are ones you left out; a company that also works in other sectors stays, 6 points down. A role whose published pay tops out a little under your minimum (within 10%) stays, 5 points down; further under, it is hidden. Each role says in one line why it matches. Your persona stays in the tab and is never sent anywhere.

      My Roles keeps the roles you mark, on this device only. Right-click a role, long-press it, or use its ⋯ button to mark it applied or save it, then set its status by hand in My Roles, in your own order, with private notes. It is kept in this browser and nowhere else: never sent anywhere, never put in the page address. Export it to keep a copy or to move it to another browser. Great match marks, while your persona is in use, the best-fitting roles among those shown: its top 2%, at most 30, each scoring 70 or more.
